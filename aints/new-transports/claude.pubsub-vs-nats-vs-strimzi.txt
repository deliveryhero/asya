● ★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────
  The transport layer in Asya is well-abstracted with 5 clean boundaries (sidecar interface, gateway client, Crossplane composition, KEDA scaler, injector). Adding any new transport
  touches all 5, but the effort varies dramatically depending on the transport's inherent complexity and operational footprint.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

  NATS is significantly less effort than Strimzi. Pub/Sub falls in between.

  Here's the breakdown across every layer that needs changes:

  Why NATS is easiest

  1. Sidecar fit: JetStream's pull consumer model maps almost 1:1 to the existing Transport interface (6 methods). It has native Ack/Nak, built-in consumer state tracking — no manual
  visibility timeout juggling like SQS, no channel reconnection dance like RabbitMQ.
  2. Infrastructure: Single binary, nats:latest with -js flag. Compare that to Kafka needing Zookeeper + brokers + the Strimzi operator CRDs.
  3. No Requeue headache: JetStream's Nak() handles redelivery natively. Kafka has no native requeue — you'd need to seek offsets (breaks ordering) or write to a retry topic (extra
  infrastructure).
  4. Test setup: One Docker container vs. Kafka's multi-container zoo.

  Why Strimzi is hardest

  - Consumer group management adds significant complexity to the sidecar
  - No native requeue — requires a retry-topic pattern or offset manipulation
  - Strimzi operator is a heavy dependency (CRDs for KafkaTopic, KafkaUser, ACLs)
  - Auth is complex — SASL/SCRAM or mTLS, both require multi-secret management in the injector and KEDA TriggerAuthentication
  - E2E testing requires deploying the full Strimzi operator into the Kind cluster

  Pub/Sub — middle ground

  Similar in shape to SQS (managed service, pull-based, SDK-driven), but adds:
  - GCP-specific auth (Workload Identity or service account JSON)
  - Cross-cloud complexity if not running on GKE
  - A separate emulator container for local testing
  - Vendor lock-in consideration

  Bottom line

  NATS ≈ 50% the effort of Strimzi, and roughly 70% the effort of Pub/Sub. If you're looking for the quickest path to a third transport, NATS JetStream is the clear winner — it's the
  lightest operationally and maps most naturally to Asya's existing transport abstraction.
